- He Lifeng – financing support for AI and new infrastructure: The vice premier called for stronger financial and factor support for artificial intelligence and computing-power networks, alongside orderly expansion of new-infrastructure investment. He also highlighted advanced manufacturing and biopharma during his Beijing inspection, relevant to the domestic AI-infrastructure and innovative-drug chains. - US n-propanol duties extended: MOFCOM said China will continue anti-dumping and countervailing duties on US-origin n-propanol from today for another five years after concluding that renewed dumping and injury were likely without the measures. The decision maintains protection for domestic chemical producers. [[Eastmoney](https://finance.eastmoney.com/a/202611053923568190.html)]

- 2027 holiday calendar – nine-day Spring Festival: The State Council set the Spring Festival break for 5–13 February, with five days for Labour Day and seven days for National Day. The longer lead time and nine-day Lunar New Year window are relevant to airlines, hotels, online travel agencies and duty-free operators. [[State Council](https://big5.www.gov.cn/gate/big5/www.gov.cn/zhengce/content/202611/content_7085101.htm)]

- AI short dramas – regulator tightens content standards: The National Radio and Television Administration reiterated that it will support live-action short dramas while regulating AI-generated productions. Platforms must maintain full human review, while AI rewriting, unauthorised remakes and misuse of likenesses or voices will face increased enforcement, raising the compliance bar for video and short-drama platforms. [[Xinhua](https://www2.xinhuanet.com/politics/20261105/b1ca375ebade4e049920243032aff783/c.html)]

- xFusion IPO – hearing scheduled: Shenzhen’s listing committee will review AI-server supplier xFusion’s ChiNext IPO on 12 November; the company plans to raise RMB80bn. First-half revenue was RMB37.29bn and net profit RMB2.11bn, with AI-server revenue accounting for 53.3% of its core business, putting the domestic server and computing-power supply chain back in focus.
