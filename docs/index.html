Morning Market Memo | 14 Sep 2026 | HK/China Pre-Open

(covers 11 Sep 2026 16:00 HKT close → 14 Sep 2026 08:31 HKT research cutoff)

  1. 01

    BRICS industrial and AI cooperation: Xi Jinping proposed five initiatives at the BRICS leaders’ meeting, including an AI open-source programme, trade and investment facilitation and a digital-industry cloud platform. China will establish a BRICS AI open-source zone and support large-model development, training and industrial applications, relevant to domestic AI, software and advanced-manufacturing names. [China Consulate]

  2. 02

    State Council backs a national computing network: Premier Li Qiang chaired a Friday-evening State Council meeting that called for a multi-level, networked computing system, stronger backbone fibre networks and closer coordination between computing capacity and electricity. The meeting also urged faster implementation of direct green-power connections and source-grid-load-storage projects, supporting data centres, optical networking, power equipment and renewable generation. [State Council]

  3. 03

    PBOC begins additional overnight liquidity operations: The central bank’s previously announced facility starts today and will run through Thursday, using fixed-rate quantity tenders with a daily ceiling of RMB600bn. The short-tenor injections are intended to meet temporary banking-system demand around the September tax-payment period. [The Paper]

  4. 04

    Digital-village plan targets chips, robotics and satellite connectivity: Three ministries released the 2026–2030 Digital Village Action Plan after Friday’s close, setting eight development indicators and 24 measures. Priorities include rural 5G and gigabit fibre, low-orbit satellite access, agricultural sensors and specialised chips, algorithms, robots, intelligent breeding and unmanned farm machinery. [Eastmoney]

  5. 05

    Brokers tighten retail algorithmic-trading access: Multiple securities firms are restricting individual clients’ connections to program-trading systems and clearing existing non-compliant links. The new industry standard prohibits “quasi-external” access, including file-interaction services, with the first remediation phase expected to finish in November. [CFi]

  6. 06

    CSRC penalises false tax-rate stories: The regulator published decisions against four people for fabricating or spreading market-moving tax information. Penalties included fines of RMB200,000–300,000 and a five-year market ban for one securities employee; one case involved an article generated with an AI tool. [Sina]

  7. 07

    Inspur Information seeks RMB9bn for AI infrastructure: Inspur Information (000977 CH) plans a private placement to no more than 35 investors. Proceeds will fund agent-oriented AI infrastructure, liquid-cooled computing clusters, multi-agent storage-computing integration, manufacturing capacity and working capital. [NBD]

  8. 08

    CATL starts its enlarged A-share buyback: CATL (300750 CH/3750 HK) bought 604,293 A-shares on Friday for approximately RMB200m at RMB330.15–331.61 per share. This was the first purchase under its RMB20bn–40bn programme to cancel shares and reduce registered capital. [Sina]

  9. 09

    Sungrow raises solar and storage prices: Sungrow Power Supply (300274 CH) confirmed that prices for photovoltaic inverters, storage converters and energy-storage systems will rise 5%–15% from September 20. The company cited higher copper, aluminium, tin, silver and gold costs and tighter supplies of core components. [NBD]

  10. 10

    PCB names deny Nvidia and Huawei supply claims: Ultrasonic Electronics (000823 CH) said it has no Nvidia supply relationship and that its 800G and 1.6T optical-module PCBs remain under development without revenue. Goldenmax International (002636 CH) said reports that it had entered Nvidia or Huawei’s supply chains were also false; its high-speed copper-clad laminates remain at laboratory or sampling stages. [Eastmoney]

  11. 11

    Huawei tri-fold phone sells out at launch: Huawei’s Mate XT 2 went on sale Saturday at RMB19,999–24,999, depending on storage and screen configuration. Huawei’s online store showed all colours and versions sold out shortly after launch, keeping attention on foldable displays, hinges and precision-component suppliers. [Eastmoney]

  12. 12

    Hongchang Electronics adds high-end PCB materials capacity: The company disclosed a proposed private placement of up to RMB1.8bn for high-end copper-clad laminates, prepreg and advanced-packaging film materials. The projects target AI servers and data-centre PCB demand, adding another capacity catalyst for the domestic high-frequency materials chain. [Eastmoney]

  13. 13

    Tencent continues HK buybacks: Tencent (700 HK) disclosed a Friday repurchase of 235,000 shares for HK$100.45m. Purchases were executed at HK$419.40–430.40 per share, maintaining the company’s regular capital-return programme. [Eastmoney]

  14. 14

    Overnight US markets – technology and China ADRs higher: The Dow gained 0.98%, the S&P 500 0.86% and Nasdaq 0.96%; the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose 1.81%, with Dell up 11.98%, Marvell 4.03%, AMD 2.49% and Intel 2.61%. The Nasdaq Golden Dragon Index added 0.40%; NIO rose 3.07%, Li Auto 1.37%, Baidu 0.89% and Alibaba 0.68%. WTI fell 2.43% to US$99.99, spot gold gained 0.75% to US$4,348.93, the DXY edged up to 99.122, and US two- and ten-year yields finished at 4.6254% and 4.9669%. [Eastmoney]

  15. 15

    US inflation reinforces expectations of a September rate increase: August CPI rose 3.4% year-on-year and 0.4% month-on-month, while core CPI increased 2.4% year-on-year and 0.3% month-on-month, versus a 0.2% monthly consensus. Traders assigned roughly a 90% probability to a September Federal Reserve increase, relevant to HK growth-stock valuations and the dollar-renminbi rate. [Eastmoney]

  16. 16

    Hormuz shipping talks postponed: Iran said a container ship was attacked near the Strait of Hormuz over the weekend, while Oman postponed a regional meeting scheduled for today on establishing a commercial shipping-security corridor. The development keeps energy producers, airlines and shipping companies sensitive to further oil and freight-rate changes. [Eastmoney]
