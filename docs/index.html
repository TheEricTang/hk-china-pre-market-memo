Morning Market Memo | 05 Oct 2026 | HK/China Pre-Open

(covers 02 Oct 2026 16:00 HKT close → 05 Oct 2026 06:35 HKT research cutoff)

  1. 01

    **US markets – tech led gains, China ADRs lagged:** The Dow rose 0.49%, S&P 500 0.73% and Nasdaq 1.19% on Friday; the Nasdaq 100 gained 1% to a record close. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index added 2.4%, Nvidia rose 1.34%, Tesla 4.65% and optical names strengthened, but Seagate and Western Digital fell about 10% on HDD-capacity concerns. The Nasdaq Golden Dragon China Index lost more than 2%, with Li Auto down 3.87% and JD.com, NetEase and Bilibili each off more than 2%. [Eastmoney] [Eastmoney]

  2. 02

    **US payrolls – September hiring slowed sharply:** Non-farm payrolls increased by just 29,000, well below expectations, while unemployment rose to 4.2% and average hourly earnings growth slowed to 3% year on year. Markets reduced the probability of another Federal Reserve rate increase in October, supporting growth stocks but leaving long-dated Treasury yields elevated. [Eastmoney]

  3. 03

    **Oil, gold and Treasuries – conflicting growth and supply signals:** The G7 agreed to release up to 100 million barrels of diesel and crude reserves over four months; WTI futures fell 1.9% on Friday while Brent was nearly flat. Spot gold dropped 0.9% to about US$4,140 an ounce and US gold futures settled 1% lower at US$4,162.30, while the ten-year Treasury yield rose roughly 4.5bp to 5.279%. The mix matters for PetroChina/CNOOC, gold miners and rate-sensitive Hong Kong property names. [Eastmoney] [Reuters] [Kiplinger]

  4. 04

    **Saudi oil facility – Houthi attack claim raises fresh supply concern:** Yemen’s Houthi movement said it used ballistic missiles and drones against a Saudi Aramco facility in Riyadh on Saturday and claimed a direct hit and fire. The Saudi-led coalition described the account as misleading, with no independently confirmed damage; the headline nevertheless adds event risk for oil, airlines and shipping after Friday’s crude decline. [Eastmoney]

  5. 05

    **US artificial intelligence policy – new federal task force:** President Trump announced a “Super Intelligence Force” on Sunday to coordinate federal-government work with technology companies, infrastructure providers, consumer groups and other stakeholders. The move reinforces US policy attention on advanced computing and AI infrastructure, relevant to Hong Kong-listed data-centre, optical-component and domestic-computing names. [Eastmoney]

  6. 06

    **G20 trade meeting – China says disputed US proposals failed to secure agreement:** MOFCOM said members agreed only on a statement opposing the weaponisation of food; proposals covering industrial overcapacity, forced labour and changes to most-favoured-nation treatment did not produce outcome documents. China said most members opposed weakening the MFN principle, maintaining a defensive official position on manufacturing exports and the multilateral trade framework. [Eastmoney]

  7. 07

    **Domestic demand – official commentary stresses a stronger home market:** A Sunday People’s Daily article said domestic demand contributed 82.1% of first-half economic growth, including 46% from final consumption. Consumer-goods trade-ins have generated more than RMB1.55 trillion of related sales and reached over 208 million participants, keeping appliances, autos, retail platforms and tourism in policy focus. [Eastmoney]

  8. 08

    **Investment mix – equipment and semiconductor spending remain priorities:** The same People’s Daily commentary said equipment-purchase investment rose 9.3% in the first eight months, intellectual-property investment increased 9.2% and high-tech industry investment grew 5.2%. Integrated-circuit manufacturing investment increased 12%, alongside continued deployment of 10-gigabit optical networks, 5G-A and satellite-internet trials. [Eastmoney]

  9. 09

    **Traditional consumer industries – branding and new retail formats encouraged:** Economic Daily highlighted the six-ministry programme covering silk, tea, ceramics, traditional Chinese medicine and brewing, estimating about 25,000 above-scale enterprises with RMB8 trillion in annual revenue. The Sunday commentary called for stronger brands, modern product design and experience-led retail formats, relevant to baijiu, TCM, tea and domestic-brand consumer companies. [Eastmoney]

  10. 10

    **October broker portfolios – healthcare drew the broadest support:** A Sunday compilation of more than 20 brokerage model portfolios identified WuXi AppTec as the most frequently recommended stock. The broader message was that the post-holiday A-share focus may shift from valuation trades toward companies able to demonstrate third-quarter earnings delivery, with healthcare among the preferred sectors. [Eastmoney]

  11. 11

    **Stock Connect – no mainland flow into today’s Hong Kong session:** HKEX’s calendar shows both northbound and southbound Stock Connect closed on Monday, 5 October, during the mainland National Day holiday. Hong Kong equities trade normally, but the absence of southbound participation removes a regular source of demand for internet, financial, telecom and high-dividend names. [HKEX]
