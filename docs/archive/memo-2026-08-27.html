Morning Market Memo | 27 Aug 2026 | HK/China Pre-Open

(covers 26 Aug 2026 16:00 HKT close → 27 Aug 2026 08:52 HKT research cutoff)

  1. 01

    Shanghai strategic industries plan: Shanghai set a 2030 target of RMB2.1tn in strategic-emerging-industry value added, with the sector exceeding 50% of large industrial output and the three lead industries—integrated circuits, biomedicine and AI—growing manufacturing output by at least 10% annually. The plan covers 12 emerging-industry tracks and 10 frontier technologies, including advanced packaging, innovative drugs, robotics, satellite internet, 6G, quantum technology and brain–computer interfaces. [Shanghai Government] [Eastmoney]

  2. 02

    PBOC liquidity: The central bank begins its previously announced overnight reverse-repo sequence today, with operations scheduled for August 27, 28 and 31 and September 1. Each operation is capped at RMB600bn, giving the PBOC a flexible tool to limit month-end volatility in overnight funding rates. [Eastmoney]

  3. 03

    Dapu Micro – major enterprise SSD order: Dapu Micro (301666 CH) disclosed an enterprise PCIe SSD order worth approximately US$515mn, or RMB3.49bn, equal to 152.7% of its audited 2025 core revenue. Delivery is due within 18 weeks, providing a fresh catalyst for domestic enterprise-storage controllers, modules and AI data-centre suppliers. [Sina]

  4. 04

    MiniMax commercialisation accelerates: MiniMax (0100 HK) reported first-half revenue of US$116.6mn, up 283.1%, with enterprise-platform revenue rising 703.1% to US$73.9mn. August annual recurring revenue exceeded US$800mn, more than 80% from enterprise business, although adjusted first-half net loss widened to US$293mn. [MiniMax] [Yicai]

  5. 05

    Zhipu opens new model on domestic chips: Zhipu released and open-sourced GLM-5.3-Flash, its first native multimodal GLM-5 model, with 320bn total parameters and 18bn activated parameters. The company said all traffic during the model’s anonymous testing phase was served by Chinese chips, offering a fresh read-through for domestic AI accelerators, interconnects and inference infrastructure. [Caixin] [Sina]

  6. 06

    Hua Hong – record interim sales: Hua Hong Semiconductor (1347 HK) reported first-half sales of US$1.378bn, up 24.5%, as wafer shipments and average selling prices increased. Profit attributable to shareholders reached US$59.6mn and gross margin improved to 14.8%, supporting the read-through for mature-node foundry utilisation and pricing. [Sina]

  7. 07

    SenseTime – first IFRS profit: SenseTime (0020 HK) reported revenue of RMB2.91bn, up 23.4%, and its first IFRS profit of RMB617mn, partly reflecting gains from AI ecosystem investments. Generative-AI revenue rose 28.2% to RMB2.33bn, nearly 80% of group sales, while the adjusted net loss narrowed 67.3% to RMB386mn and overseas revenue grew 127%. [STCN] [SenseTime]

  8. 08

    Meitu – subscriptions drive growth: Meitu (1357 HK) reported first-half revenue of RMB2.21bn, up 22.1%, and attributable profit of RMB619mn, up 40%. Paying subscribers increased 19.7% to more than 18.44mn, with overseas additions and productivity applications becoming the main growth drivers. [STCN]

  9. 09

    Lianxun Instruments – optical test demand: Lianxun Instruments reported first-half revenue of RMB1.53bn, up 208.7%, and net profit of RMB567mn, up 903%, citing accelerating AI, data-centre and high-speed optical-communications demand. The company also proposed a 4.8-for-10 capitalisation issue, keeping optical-component and test-equipment peers in focus. [Eastmoney] [Eastmoney]

  10. 10

    CNOOC raises interim payout: CNOOC (0883 HK) reported first-half revenue of RMB242.66bn, up 16.9%, and attributable profit of RMB85.82bn, up 23.4%. The board declared an interim dividend of HK$0.94 per share, relevant for the oil major’s yield profile despite softer overnight crude prices. [Eastmoney]

  11. 11

    China National Heavy Duty Truck: China National Heavy Duty Truck (3808 HK) reported first-half revenue of RMB70.84bn, up 39.2%, and net profit of RMB4.33bn, up 26.2%. The result provides a read-through for heavy-truck demand, commercial-vehicle components and domestic infrastructure-linked transport activity. [Eastmoney]

  12. 12

    Hong Kong capital markets: The SFC’s April–June quarterly report said southbound trading extended its record run of consecutive monthly net inflows, while Stock Connect turnover represented a material share of Hong Kong cash-market activity. The regulator also described second-quarter IPO fundraising as active, supportive for HKEX, brokers and listing-service providers. [Eastmoney]

  13. 13

    Apple event set for September 9: Apple scheduled its autumn product launch for September 9 US time, with the market focused on its first foldable iPhone. The event will be the first under incoming CEO John Ternus after Tim Cook moves to executive chairman on September 1, putting HK/A-listed Apple suppliers and foldable-display components back in focus. [Eastmoney] [Eastmoney]

  14. 14

    Nvidia beats and guides above consensus: Nvidia reported fiscal second-quarter revenue of US$96.2bn, up 106%, versus roughly US$92.2bn expected; data-centre revenue reached US$89bn and adjusted EPS was US$2.22. Third-quarter revenue guidance centred on US$108bn, excluding China data-centre compute sales, and the shares recovered to gain more than 4% after hours—positive for HK/A AI servers, PCB, optics and memory names. [Yicai] [Eastmoney]

  15. 15

    Overnight US markets and China ADRs: The Dow fell 0.21%, the S&P 500 slipped 0.02% and the Nasdaq lost 0.08%; the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index added 0.20%, with Qualcomm up 1.97% and Western Digital up more than 4%, while Tesla fell 1.26%. The Nasdaq Golden Dragon China Index declined 0.61%; NetEase lost 4.2%, Bilibili 2.53% and PDD 1.1%, while Alibaba gained 0.33% and XPeng 0.95%. [Yicai] [Eastmoney]

  16. 16

    US inflation and cross-assets: July core PCE rose 0.2% month on month and 3.3% year on year, both in line with consensus, while headline monthly inflation was slightly firmer than expected. The US 10-year yield rose about 3bp to 4.658%, DXY gained 0.25% to 99.165, COMEX gold fell 0.99% to US$4,647.8/oz and Brent declined 0.84% to US$87.84/bbl; higher yields temper the Nvidia read-through, while softer oil is mildly favourable for airlines and downstream users. [Eastmoney] [Eastmoney]
