Morning Market Memo | 19 Aug 2026 | HK/China Pre-Open

(covers 18 Aug 2026 16:00 HKT close → 19 Aug 2026 07:12 HKT research cutoff)

  1. 01

    Housing provident funds – broader access from 20 September: Premier Li Qiang signed revised rules removing the income-ratio threshold for rental withdrawals and adding home renovation and property-management fees as eligible uses. Flexible workers may contribute voluntarily, while nationwide record recognition and faster loan approvals should support housing consumption, property services and mortgage activity. [Eastmoney]

  2. 02

    Abnormal trading – SSE suspends investor accounts: The Shanghai Stock Exchange said some investors trading Aili Home (603221 CH) had disrupted normal market order and were subjected to account suspensions. The stock rose 214.8% between 21 July and 18 August despite repeated risk notices and two trading halts, signalling tighter scrutiny of heavily promoted concept stocks. [Eastmoney]

  3. 03

    Landun Photoelectron – trading halt after five limit-ups: Landun Photoelectron (300862 CH) will be suspended from today for a review expected to last no more than three trading days. The shares gained 197.2% over five sessions following its proposed acquisition of Suzhou Lanchuang Technology, which remains subject to approvals and could be delayed or cancelled. [Eastmoney]

  4. 04

    Shenzhen New Star – aluminium-materials expansion: Shenzhen New Star (603978 CH) plans to invest RMB400m in the first phase of a Zhejiang project with annual capacity of 20,000 tonnes of high-end aluminium-based materials. The broader site is planned for 100,000 tonnes, adding a fresh capacity catalyst for specialty aluminium and advanced-material suppliers. [Eastmoney]

  5. 05

    LPSK – acquisition adds automation exposure: LPSK (002333 CH) signed an agreement to acquire 51% of Mengying Technology for cash. The target makes automated material-handling components used in advanced display-panel factories, giving LPSK an entry into intelligent equipment while creating internal demand for its aluminium extrusion products. [Eastmoney]

  6. 06

    Jintian Copper – magnet unit considered for spin-off: Jintian Copper said it is planning a separate listing for controlled subsidiary Ketian Magnetics. The proposal gives the rare-earth permanent-magnet chain a fresh capital-markets catalyst, although the timetable and final structure remain subject to subsequent announcements. [Eastmoney]

  7. 07

    Tianfu Communication – AI optics demand supports earnings: Tianfu Communication (300394 CH) reported first-half revenue of RMB2.83bn, up 15.2%, and net profit of RMB1.20bn, up 33.9%, with a proposed RMB5 dividend per ten shares. Management attributed growth to data-centre and AI-supercomputing demand, while noting that shortages of certain materials constrained the pace of expansion. [Eastmoney]

  8. 08

    Focus Media – profit growth despite softer revenue: Focus Media (002027 CH) reported first-half revenue of RMB5.99bn, down 2.0%, while net profit rose 17.4% to RMB3.13bn. The divergence provides an overnight read-through on advertiser demand and operating discipline across consumer-facing media names. [Eastmoney]

  9. 09

    Fuyao Glass – higher-value products offset by weaker profit: Fuyao Glass (600660 CH) posted first-half revenue of RMB21.97bn, up 2.4%, but net profit fell 17.4% to RMB3.97bn; it proposed a cash dividend of RMB1 per share. The company said a larger contribution from higher-value products supported sales, while foreign-exchange effects weighed on earnings, relevant to the A/H auto-glass name. [Eastmoney]

  10. 10

    Huayou Cobalt – battery-material shipments expand: Huayou Cobalt (603799 CH) reported first-half revenue of RMB55.57bn and net profit of RMB3.51bn, up 49.4% and 29.4%, respectively. Ternary cathode shipments reached 76,300 tonnes and precursor shipments 80,800 tonnes, with ultra-high-nickel products exceeding 70% of cathode volumes. [Eastmoney]

  11. 11

    Espressif – chairman proposes buyback: Espressif Systems (688018 CH) plans a RMB100m–RMB200m share repurchase for employee ownership or equity incentives, subject to board approval. The proposed ceiling is RMB135 per share, providing a fresh capital-return signal for the domestic IoT and connectivity-chip complex. [Eastmoney]

  12. 12

    Yaka Technology – RMB868m computing-services procurement: Yaka Technology’s (301085 CH) subsidiary signed two general-computing service and network-product agreements worth RMB434m each, including tax. The RMB868m commitment highlights continued infrastructure spending by domestic computing-service operators, although the supplier was not identified. [Eastmoney]

  13. 13

    Power grid orders – South Grid awards: Han Cable (002498 CH) estimated that it won RMB717m of China Southern Power Grid framework orders, equivalent to about 6.9% of its 2025 revenue. Beijing Creative Distribution Automation (002350 CH) separately estimated RMB304m of awards, around 14.2% of its prior-year revenue, supporting the grid-equipment order pipeline. [Eastmoney]

  14. 14

    PCB equipment – Dongwei signs RMB163m contract: Dongwei Technology (688700 CH) signed a RMB163m sales contract covering vertical continuous copper-plating lines and associated software. The company expects the order to contribute positively to 2027 results, providing a fresh catalyst for PCB and advanced-plating equipment suppliers. [Eastmoney]

  15. 15

    Overnight US markets – AI hardware and China ADRs lower: The Dow fell 0.22%, S&P 500 0.69% and Nasdaq 1.33%. SanDisk, SK Hynix and Seagate lost more than 9%, Western Digital and Micron over 7%, Coherent over 12%, Lumentum over 9% and Nvidia 2.34%; the Nasdaq Golden Dragon Index fell 1.01%, led by Baidu down 12.73%, while JD rose 1.12%. WTI gained 0.81% to US$84.42, but COMEX gold fell 1.88% and silver 4.24%, relevant to HK optical, storage, internet, energy and precious-metals names. [Eastmoney]

  16. 16

    Iran/Hormuz – conflicting overnight statements: President Trump said the US was not holding talks with Iran, the maritime blockade remained in force and the Strait of Hormuz was open. Iran’s chief negotiator separately said the strait would remain closed until the US lifted its port blockade and oil sanctions, unfroze assets and stopped military threats, leaving oil-shipping uncertainty unresolved. [Eastmoney]
