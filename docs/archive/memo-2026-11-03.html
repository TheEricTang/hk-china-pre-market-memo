Morning Market Memo | 03 Nov 2026 | HK/China Pre-Open

(covers 02 Nov 2026 16:00 HKT close → 03 Nov 2026 06:10 HKT research cutoff)

  1. 01

    PBOC – third swap-facility operation: The central bank will conduct its third securities, funds and insurers swap-facility operation today, with a one-year extendable term. Thirty-one securities and fund firms applied for more than RMB100bn; the previous two operations totalled RMB105bn. [Xinhua]

  2. 02

    Buyback relending becomes permanent: The PBOC, financial regulator and CSRC converted the stock-buyback and shareholder-increase relending programme from a temporary measure into a long-term arrangement, cutting the relending rate from 1.25% to 1%. Supported loans signed have reached about RMB440bn, with more than RMB280bn disbursed. [Eastmoney]

  3. 03

    CSRC – buyback and margin rules: The regulator plans to add trading below the IPO price as a trigger for stabilisation buybacks, remove the six-month listing requirement and allow orders during opening and closing auctions. It will also review financing-margin ratios and other risk controls, with scope to release additional margin-financing capacity. [CSRC]

  4. 04

    Active ETFs – deployment to accelerate: China’s first 18 active ETFs have raised more than RMB35bn since subscriptions opened on October 23; 16 reached their RMB2bn individual fundraising caps. The CSRC said completed funds should accelerate portfolio construction, providing a fresh source of A-share demand. [CSRC]

  5. 05

    HKEX – RMB bonds accepted as derivatives collateral: HKEX’s futures and options clearing houses began accepting China government bonds, policy-bank bonds held through Bond Connect and offshore Ministry of Finance bonds as non-cash margin collateral. Seven institutions posted more than RMB400m on the first day, improving capital efficiency and widening the use of offshore RMB assets. [HKEX]

  6. 06

    Alibaba – Qwen sells consumer AI capacity through livestreaming: A promotional Qwen Office monthly membership priced at RMB0.01 sold nearly 100,000 units in Li Jiaqi’s livestream within 30 minutes, representing roughly two trillion tokens of usage allowance. The campaign is a user-acquisition exercise rather than meaningful near-term revenue, but marks AI services moving into mainstream Double 11 retail channels. [Eastmoney]

  7. 07

    Dahua – RMB2.63bn China Mobile AI order: Dahua Technology’s subsidiary Huaji Zhihui was selected for about RMB2.63bn of China Mobile’s 2026–27 next-generation AI supernode procurement, covering 4,096 of the project’s 20,480 accelerator cards. The amount exceeded Dahua’s previously approved RMB2.05bn related-party transaction estimate; final contract and revenue timing remain subject to implementation. [CNInfo]

  8. 08

    Far East Smarter Energy – data-centre cable orders: The company reported RMB1.498bn of October contracts worth at least RMB10m each, up 41.0% year on year. The total included RMB277m of North American computing-centre cable orders, RMB200m of smart-manufacturing cable orders and RMB255m from State Grid projects. [Eastmoney]

  9. 09

    CATL – RMB5.36bn spent on current buyback: CATL had repurchased 19.61m A-shares through October 31, equal to 0.4447% of its A-share capital, at prices between RMB218.79 and RMB331.61. The wider programme authorises RMB20bn–40bn of purchases for cancellation, relevant to both 300750 CH and 3750 HK. [Sina]

  10. 10

    A-share capital actions – AI/data names: Xiechuang Data’s controlling shareholder proposed buying RMB500m–1bn of shares over six months, while Xingyun Technology’s chairman proposed a RMB200m–300m company buyback. Both announcements arrived after the close and add to the regulator-backed increase in corporate capital support. [21st Century Business Herald]

  11. 11

    Pop Mart – buyback continued: Pop Mart repurchased 360,000 shares for HK$48.90m on Monday at HK$135.30–136.50 per share, with the stock intended for cancellation. It has bought 2.01m shares across the latest three disclosed sessions, providing fresh support for 9992 HK. [Investing.com]

  12. 12

    AI optics – Marvell to acquire Lumentum: Marvell agreed to buy Lumentum in a US$115bn all-share transaction, offering 4.5 Marvell shares per Lumentum share and a 23.9% premium to unaffected prices. The companies target more than US$1bn of annual cost synergies and a first-half 2028 close; Marvell fell 12.72% and Lumentum 18.59%, setting a weak overnight read-through for optical and CPO names. [Lumentum]

  13. 13

    Apple – headline beat, China below consensus: Fiscal fourth-quarter revenue rose 12% to US$114.5bn and EPS reached US$2.10; iPhone revenue was US$57.58bn. Greater China revenue increased 21% to US$17.54bn but missed the US$18.75bn consensus, while shares fell 2.6% during the earnings call, leaving a mixed read-through for Hong Kong-listed suppliers. [Apple]

  14. 14

    Overnight US markets: The Dow rose 0.41%, while the S&P 500 fell 0.51%, Nasdaq 1.07% and SOX 3.37%; Intel lost 8.8%, AMD 7.52%, Seagate 7.88%, Western Digital 7.52%, Micron 5.78% and Nvidia 1.26%. The Golden Dragon index gained 2.25%, led by Bilibili +6.55%, XPeng +5.92% and Alibaba +3.54%. WTI fell 7.36% to US$96.46, Brent 7.63% to US$104.77, COMEX gold rose 1.48% to US$3,956.60, DXY fell 0.16% to 101.719 and the US 10-year yield ended at 4.9125%. [Eastmoney]

  15. 15

    Southbound flow – selling continued: Mainland investors recorded HK$4.47bn of net southbound selling on Monday on HK$75.12bn of turnover. Xiaomi saw the largest net sale at about HK$1.80bn, while CNOOC and Zhipu recorded net buying of HK$888m and HK$365m respectively. [Eastmoney]
