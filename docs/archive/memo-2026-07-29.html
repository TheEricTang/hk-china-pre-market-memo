Morning Market Memo | 29 Jul 2026 | HK/China Pre-Open

(covers 28 Jul 2026 16:00 HKT close → 29 Jul 2026 07:46 HKT research cutoff)

  1. 01

    Overnight US markets – mixed close, hardware pressure: The Dow rose 1.03%, S&P 500 gained 0.21% and Nasdaq fell 0.22%. Optical and memory names sold off sharply: SanDisk and Kioxia ADR lost more than 14%, Corning 12%, Coherent 10%, Micron and SK Hynix about 9%, while the Nasdaq Golden Dragon Index gained 1.08%, with JD.com above 2% and NIO and Bilibili above 1%. WTI fell 4.21% to US$79.13, COMEX gold lost 1.18% to US$4,028.80 and silver fell 2.35% to US$57.34. [Eastmoney]

  2. 02

    Seagate – earnings beat after storage sell-off: Seagate rose more than 7% after hours after reporting fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of US$3.65bn and adjusted EPS of US$5.71, both above consensus. It guided first-quarter revenue to US$4.0bn–US$4.2bn versus the US$3.79bn estimate, providing a partial offset for the overnight memory and storage rout. [Eastmoney]

  3. 03

    Kumamoto earthquake – semiconductor operations restarting: A magnitude-7.1 earthquake prompted TSMC’s JASM plant to evacuate staff and assess its facilities. TSMC later said the structure was safe and production lines were gradually restarting, although some construction work remained suspended; the incident keeps Japanese semiconductor equipment and materials supply under watch. [Eastmoney] [Reuters]

  4. 04

    China–Turkey EV dispute – WTO panel backs Beijing: The WTO released its panel report on Turkey’s restrictions covering Chinese electric vehicles and other vehicles. China’s Commerce Ministry said the panel supported China’s claims and found the measures inconsistent with WTO rules, relevant to Chinese automakers’ overseas market access. [Yicai]

  5. 05

    PBOC liquidity – overnight operations begin today: The PBOC is scheduled to conduct overnight reverse-repurchase operations from July 29–31 and again on August 3 through fixed-rate, quantity tenders. The additional tool is intended to smooth cross-month funding conditions alongside regular open-market operations. [Eastmoney]

  6. 06

    Credit demand – property and consumer loans remain soft: PBOC second-quarter loan data showed outstanding property loans at RMB50.74tn, down 4.9% year on year. Consumer loans excluding mortgages stood at RMB20.83tn, down 1.7%, keeping banks, developers and discretionary-consumption names sensitive to further demand-support measures. [Eastmoney]

  7. 07

    Shanghai – future industries and consumption policy: Shanghai’s municipal finance commission reviewed its 2026–30 plans for building an international consumption centre and developing future industries. Officials called for faster ecosystem formation in strategically contested technologies and stronger consumption’s contribution to growth, relevant to Shanghai-based technology, retail and services companies. [Yicai]

  8. 08

    AI infrastructure – IPv6 upgrade campaign launched: The Cyberspace Administration said a one-year campaign will push leading generative-AI applications to support IPv6, improve infrastructure compatibility and raise the IPv6 share of model API traffic. Beijing, Shanghai, Zhejiang and Shenzhen authorities are participating with five leading model developers. [CAC]

  9. 09

    Zhongji Innolight – RMB40bn–80bn buyback proposed: Chairman and president Liu Sheng proposed that Zhongji Innolight (300308 CH) repurchase A-shares through competitive trading for employee ownership or equity incentives. The proposed ceiling is 150% of the 30-session average before board approval; the unusually large programme follows heavy volatility across global optical-networking names. [Yicai] [Eastmoney]

  10. 10

    BOE – controlling shareholder plans RMB500m–1bn purchase: BOE Technology’s (000725 CH) controlling shareholder Beijing Electronics Holding plans to increase its A-share stake by RMB500m–1bn without a fixed purchase-price range. The post-close announcement is a fresh support signal for the display-panel and electronics complex. [Eastmoney]

  11. 11

    ChangXin Technology – MSCI inclusion: MSCI will add newly listed ChangXin Technology (688825 CH) to the MSCI China All Shares Index effective August 10. The post-close decision creates an upcoming passive-flow catalyst for the domestic memory-chip leader. [Eastmoney]

  12. 12

    China State Construction – RMB22.4bn Kuwait contract: China State Construction (601668 CH) signed the North Kabd wastewater-treatment plant and supporting-project contract in Kuwait for KWD999.85m, approximately RMB22.4bn. The agreement covers five years of design and construction followed by five years of maintenance and operation. [Yicai] [Eastmoney]

  13. 13

    Jiuzhou Yigui – semiconductor equipment investment: Jiuzhou Yigui’s (688485 CH) subsidiary plans to invest up to RMB630m in an advanced semiconductor-wafer laser stealth-dicing project, including as much as RMB600m for equipment. The production line is targeted for completion and gradual commissioning in the first quarter of 2027. [Eastmoney]

  14. 14

    Wanma – RMB820m cable-material expansion: Wanma (002276 CH) plans a RMB450m plant capable of producing 100,000 tonnes of cable materials and a separate RMB370m Qingdao submarine-cable insulation-material project. Construction is scheduled across 2026–28, adding capacity tied to power-grid and offshore-cable investment. [Eastmoney]

  15. 15

    Kingsoft Office – headline profit boosted by investments: Kingsoft Office (688111 CH) expects first-half revenue of RMB3.21bn–3.41bn, up 21%–28%, and net profit of RMB2.32bn–2.72bn, up 210%–264%. The company said investment-fund returns made a significant contribution, so the headline profit growth overstates the underlying operating improvement. [Eastmoney]

  16. 16

    Shenghui Integration – RMB858m electronics-factory contract: Shenghui Integration (603163 CH) was selected for an RMB858m, pre-tax clean-room and facility engineering project for Qingding Precision Electronics in Huai’an. The order is a fresh catalyst for semiconductor and precision-electronics factory engineering suppliers. [Eastmoney]
