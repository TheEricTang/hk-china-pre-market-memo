Morning Market Memo | 02 Oct 2026 | HK/China Pre-Open

(covers 30 Sep 2026 16:00 HKT close → 02 Oct 2026 06:45 HKT research cutoff)

  1. 01

    Fiscal policy – incremental measures under study: Finance Minister Lan Fo’an said the ministry will formulate targeted incremental policies, accelerate spending and focus more on domestic demand. Measures include expanding consumption and investment interest subsidies, using unused local-government debt ceilings and issuing RMB300bn of special treasury bonds to replenish core Tier-1 capital at central financial institutions. [Eastmoney]

  2. 02

    Wuhan housing rules – completed-home sales and funding flexibility: Wuhan’s new implementation rules give priority to completed-home sales on land transferred after 28 August; projects using presales generally must reach structural topping-out. Eligible developers may replace up to 30% of required completion funds with bank guarantees, while deposits on completed homes are generally capped at 5% of the purchase price. [Wuhan Housing Bureau] [Yicai]

  3. 03

    Golden Week travel – rail outperforms overall flows: The transport ministry estimated 334.1m cross-regional trips on 1 October, up 52.3% from the previous day but 0.5% below last year’s comparable day. Rail traffic was expected to reach 24.8m, up 7.2% year on year, while air passenger volume was estimated at 2.51m, up 0.3%; the mix is relevant to airlines, rail operators, hotels and online travel platforms. [Beijing Daily]

  4. 04

    Huawei Mate 90 – flagship launch during the HK holiday: Huawei launched and began selling the Mate 90 series from RMB5,999, with the top 2TB RS model priced at RMB18,999. The range uses Kirin 9030, 9035 and 9050 Pro chips; Huawei also said devices running HarmonyOS 6 or 7 have exceeded 90m, providing a fresh catalyst for the domestic semiconductor, optics and handset-component chains. [Securities Times] [Huawei]

  5. 05

    BYD – September exports offset softer year-to-date volume: BYD (1211 HK) sold 463,561 new-energy vehicles in September, up 17.0% year on year, including 273,143 battery-electric passenger cars, up 33.2%, and 183,570 plug-in hybrids, down 2.4%. Exports reached 180,700 units; January–September sales were 3.13m, still 3.9% below the prior-year period. [Sina] [IT Home]

  6. 06

    XPeng and NIO – September deliveries rose: XPeng (9868 HK) delivered 41,256 vehicles in September, up 5% month on month, taking third-quarter deliveries to 118,390, up 15% sequentially. NIO (9866 HK) delivered 37,408 vehicles, up 7.7% year on year; third-quarter deliveries increased 25.4% to 109,178. [XPeng] [NIO]

  7. 07

    Other EV makers – Leapmotor retains the volume lead: Leapmotor (9863 HK) delivered 105,656 vehicles globally in September, up 59%, including more than 27,000 exports. Xiaomi (1810 HK) reported more than 40,000 deliveries, Zeekr delivered 37,216 vehicles, up 103.9%, and Li Auto (2015 HK) delivered 31,817; the figures were released while Hong Kong was closed on Thursday. [Eastmoney]

  8. 08

    Tencent – buyback continued into the holiday: Tencent (700 HK) repurchased 234,000 shares on 30 September for about HK$100.3m at HK$425.00–432.20 per share. It was the company’s 33rd consecutive repurchase session since 17 August, taking purchases over that stretch to 12.02m shares for HK$5.31bn. [Securities Times]

  9. 09

    Overnight US markets – semis and optics led, China ADRs lagged: The Dow rose 0.04%, the S&P 500 gained 0.19% and the Nasdaq added 0.04%; the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index advanced 1.59%. Micron rose 3.03%, SanDisk 2.75%, Seagate 2.52% and Coherent 10.9%, while the Nasdaq Golden Dragon Index fell 1.03%, with Li Auto down 2.11% and PDD down 1.85%. WTI gained 2.71%, Brent 4.37%, and the US 10-year yield retreated after touching 5.342%. [CLS]

  10. 10

    Middle East – oil rises on renewed US escalation signals: Reports said Washington was considering sending up to 10,000 additional troops and a third aircraft carrier to the Middle East. President Trump said he would soon decide whether Iran signs an agreement or faces further action; the resulting oil move is relevant to CNOOC, PetroChina, Sinopec, airlines and shipping names. [CLS]

  11. 11

    Anthropic – reported IPO timetable returns: Anthropic is reportedly considering launching an IPO roadshow during the week of 9 November, with trading potentially beginning before the 26 November US Thanksgiving holiday. The timing remains subject to change, but a large AI listing would provide a fresh valuation reference for listed Chinese cloud, model and computing-infrastructure companies. [CLS]

  12. 12

    Nike – weak outlook for global sportswear demand: Nike reported fiscal first-quarter revenue of US$11.21bn versus a US$11.33bn estimate and EPS of US$0.48, while forecasting a high-single-digit revenue decline for fiscal 2027. Gross margin improved to 42.8% and inventory was slightly below expectations at US$7.8bn; the demand outlook is a read-through for Anta Sports, Li Ning and footwear suppliers. [CLS]
