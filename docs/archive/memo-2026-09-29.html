Morning Market Memo | 29 Sep 2026 | HK/China Pre-Open

(covers 28 Sep 2026 16:00 HKT close → 29 Sep 2026 06:39 HKT research cutoff)

  1. 01

    Macro policy – incremental measures: Premier Li Qiang’s State Council meeting called for stronger countercyclical adjustment, faster bond deployment and use of unused local-government debt quotas. Officials will consider adjusting monetary tools, expanding relending for technology and smaller businesses, stabilising property and supporting employment and household income. [Eastmoney]

  2. 02

    Service-sector finance – 19 measures: The PBOC, CSRC and six other agencies instructed financial institutions to reduce reliance on physical collateral and improve financing for light-asset businesses, SMEs and sole proprietors. Measures cover technology services, logistics, tourism, healthcare, supply-chain finance, M&A, listings and REITs. [Eastmoney]

  3. 03

    PBOC liquidity – RMB1.1tn gross injection: The central bank conducted RMB139bn of seven-day reverse repos at 1.4%, RMB661bn of overnight repos and RMB300bn of 14-day funding. With RMB660.3bn of repos and RMB600bn of MLF maturing, the day still produced a RMB160.3bn net drain; September’s MLF operations delivered a RMB200bn net injection. [Eastmoney] [NBD]

  4. 04

    Central SOEs – RMB2tn “six networks” investment: SASAC said the new five-year plan targets a further five-percentage-point increase in strategic emerging industries’ share of central-SOE value added by 2030. Central SOEs have scheduled about RMB2tn of 2026 investment across the six national networks, supporting infrastructure, power-grid, transport and equipment names. [Eastmoney]

  5. 05

    Shanghai housing – tighter presale rules: Shanghai’s new six-part housing-sales framework took effect Monday, covering presales, completed-home sales, lead-bank arrangements, financing and quality supervision. Projects tied to land notices issued after 28 August may presell only after the relevant building has topped out, with presale proceeds subject to full-process supervision. [Eastmoney]

  6. 06

    GAC restructuring – buying FAW Toyota stake: GAC Group plans to issue shares to acquire FAW’s 50% holding in FAW Toyota and may raise supporting funds from up to 35 investors. The shares resume trading today, making the proposed Toyota joint-venture consolidation the main fresh catalyst for GAC’s A and H shares. [Eastmoney]

  7. 07

    ChangXin Memory – RMB34.9bn expansion: ChangXin Technology approved a RMB24.1bn advanced-DRAM research project and a RMB10.8bn second-phase wafer-testing base. The 30-month programme targets process upgrades and additional in-house back-end capacity, with read-through for domestic semiconductor equipment, testing and materials suppliers. [Eastmoney]

  8. 08

    Xinlian Integrated – nine-month profit guidance: The foundry expects January–September revenue of about RMB7.77bn, up 43.2%, and attributable profit of roughly RMB681m versus a loss a year earlier. Management cited AI applications, technology upgrades and domestic substitution as drivers of scale expansion. [Eastmoney] [Eastmoney]

  9. 09

    CATL/Hunan Yuneng – stake cut completed: CATL disclosed completion of its planned sale of about 17.46m Hunan Yuneng shares, equal to roughly 2.06% of the battery-materials producer. CATL’s remaining interest is just under 5%, removing it from the above-5% shareholder threshold while leaving a fresh supply-overhang read-through for Hunan Yuneng. [Eastmoney]

  10. 10

    CITIC Securities – chairman transition: Zhang Youjun retired as chairman and legal representative, while executive director and former general manager Zou Yingguang was elected chairman. Zou will also perform the general manager’s duties until a replacement is appointed. [Eastmoney]

  11. 11

    Zhongji Innolight – RMB1.33bn interim dividend: The optical-module maker will pay RMB1.20 per A share, based on roughly 1.11bn eligible shares, with 9 October as the record date and 12 October as the ex-dividend date. The distribution reinforces shareholder-return support after strong 1.6T-product shipments. [Eastmoney]

  12. 12

    Jingwang Electronics – Hong Kong debut: The PCB producer begins trading in Hong Kong today after selling 72.94m H shares at HK$69.88 each. The dual listing adds a new AI-server, high-end PCB and automotive-electronics name to the Hong Kong market. [Eastmoney]

  13. 13

    Tencent – HK$100m buyback: Tencent repurchased 227,000 shares Monday at HK$438.80–446.80 per share, with the stock scheduled for cancellation. The company has now repurchased about 47.62m shares under the May authorisation. [Eastmoney] [Sina]

  14. 14

    Nvidia – record-sized repurchase authorisation: Nvidia added US$150bn to its buyback authority, lifting remaining capacity to US$235bn for execution through fiscal 2028. The shares gained 1.68% despite the broader semiconductor decline, offering limited support for Hong Kong’s AI-hardware complex. [Eastmoney]

  15. 15

    US markets – technology and semis lower, China ADRs firmer: The Dow fell 0.67%, S&P 500 0.77% and Nasdaq 0.92%. ARM lost more than 8%, Qualcomm more than 7%, SK Hynix and Intel more than 5%, while AMD, SanDisk and Marvell fell over 3%; the Nasdaq Golden Dragon China Index nevertheless gained 1.12%, led by NetEase +4.88%, KE Holdings +3.80% and Li Auto +1.83%. [Eastmoney]

  16. 16

    Cross-assets – metals tumble while oil rises: WTI gained 0.95% to US$93.29 a barrel, but COMEX gold dropped 4.00% to US$4,148.50 and silver fell 5.82% to US$61.03. Separately, a US official said Qatar-mediated US–Iran discussions could resume around Iran’s proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, keeping energy names sensitive to headlines. [Eastmoney] [Eastmoney]

    Source availability – Eastmoney’s same-day 经济早餐 had not appeared in its column index or date-specific search by the 06:39 HKT cutoff; the 06:15 Choice global-markets wrap was used for final overnight closes. [Eastmoney]
